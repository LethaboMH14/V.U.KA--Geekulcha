## 3. Fifteen hostile questions — spoken answers

1. **“Is this not just surveillance with extra steps?”** “It is sensing, and it affects people who did not buy it. That is why a nonmember's access and challenge route belongs in the product. Those controls are not built here yet, so we cannot call this ready for a real-data pilot.”

2. **“How is this different from a panic-button app?”** “The founding problem is that coercion removes the private moment needed to press a button. Hands-free initiation is the proposed difference. We still have to demonstrate that it works without exposing the person.”

3. **“You said less AI reliance, but your architecture is full of models.”** “Models are uncertain inputs. The human decision gate, sensitive-change approvals and record checks do not need a model to decide authority. We should be judged on whether those boundaries actually hold.”

4. **“Why trust a blockchain claim from a student team?”** “You should not trust it because we say blockchain. Ask for the package and verify the commitment independently. It can establish existence by a time, not whether an accusation was true.”

5. **“Your forecast fails. Why is the rest not equally broken?”** “The forecast loses to a constant baseline: MAE 0.484 against 0.246. That is why we exclude its prediction claim. Every other component needs its own evidence; one passing test does not rescue another failing one.”

6. **“Who is liable when this gets someone hurt?”** “We cannot allocate liability by presentation. A real service needs defined responsible parties, response agreements, insurance and legal review. Those arrangements are not evidenced here, so we are not offering operational protection.”

7. **“What stops police demanding access?”** “We need lawful-process review, scoped access and an accountable information officer. We cannot promise to ignore a lawful order, and we should not hold data merely because someone might demand it later.”

8. **“What stops an acquisition reversing your promises?”** “No sentence in a deck can guarantee that. Minimisation, independent exports, contractual restrictions and an exit/deletion process reduce the risk. Ownership and governance protections still need legal work.”

9. **“Is the system live?”** “This clean checkout currently contains the design and repository controls. We distinguish any later working demonstration from simulation and specification. Historical results have not been reproduced here.”

10. **“Are the face thresholds calibrated?”** “No. The supplied thresholds are targets, and demographic bias evaluation has not run. We will not attach a precision claim to them.”

11. **“Does a timestamp prove the victim was coerced?”** “No. It can help check what the service recorded and when its commitment existed. Intent and the truth of the event need other evidence and human judgment.”

12. **“How do you delete immutable data?”** “We propose deleting held payloads and links, while carefully reviewing what proof residue remains. A hash can still be linkable personal information. The deletion route is unbuilt and its legal basis unresolved.”

13. **“Why R299?”** “It is a supplied model, not a tested market price. Hardware, support and cloud assumptions produce a gross figure; payment costs, acquisition, tax and upfront cash can materially change it.”

14. **“Which two subsystems reached TRL 5?”** “The brief reports two but does not name them or include their evidence. We will not guess. Our current statement is a TRL 4 target with historical verification awaiting reproduction.”

15. **“Why seven people and still no appliance?”** “Roles do not manufacture hardware. The appliance is not fabricated, and measured power and integration remain assigned work. That limitation belongs on the same page as the team.”
