# VUKA — Everything It Does, In Plain Words

✅ built · 🔨 building in the next 9 days · 📋 designed, on the roadmap

---

## The whole thing in one sentence a child would understand

> **A phone that notices when you're in trouble even if you can't reach it, a street that remembers who belongs there, and a notebook nobody can secretly rewrite — not even us.**

Three parts. The phone watches out for **you**. The street watches out for **everyone**. The notebook makes sure **we can be checked**.

---

## PART 1 · VUKA on your phone — it looks after one person

- ✅ **It listens for trouble without recording you.** Your phone hears the *shape* of a sound — breaking glass, a gunshot, shouting — the way you'd recognise a dog's bark through a wall without knowing what dog it is. It throws the sound away and keeps only the word for it.
- ✅ **It notices when your phone stops talking to your car.** If your phone is connected to your car and suddenly they're separated while the car is moving — that's what a hijacking looks like from the inside. You never pressed a button. You never opened an app.
- ✅ **It feels how you're moving.** Walking, driving, running, being still, being carried — the phone can tell these apart.
- ✅ **It knows the situation, not just the noise.** Load-shedding stage 4? Dark outside? Two in the morning? A street where things have happened before? The same sound means something different at 02:00 in the dark than at 14:00 in the sun.
- ✅ **It adds up its senses like a careful person, not a nervous one.** Three separate senses — ears, movement, car connection. If they all agree, it takes it seriously. **If they disagree with each other, it goes quiet and asks instead of shouting.** A dog barking and a thunderclap are noise. A crash *and* movement *and* your car just disconnected is a story.
- ✅ **It has a secret PIN that lies for you.** Type the real number and your phone unlocks. Type the panic number and your phone *also* unlocks — looking completely normal — while quietly telling your people you're in trouble.
- ✅ **It can pretend to be off.** Screen black, looks dead, still awake.
- ✅ **It works when nothing else does.** No signal, no electricity, no Wi-Fi, screen off, all night. The thinking happens inside the phone, not on the internet.
- ✅ **It always gives you a moment to say "no, I'm fine."** Every single alert has a visible countdown you can cancel. Nothing leaves without that window passing.
- ✅ **It tells your Guardians, not a stranger.** The people you chose, first.
- ✅ **It forgets.** After a while, it stops carrying what happened. Nobody has to decide to forgive you.

## PART 2 · BEACON on your street — it looks after a whole neighbourhood

- ✅ **It learns who belongs before it learns who doesn't.** The neighbour, the domestic worker, the delivery guy who comes every Tuesday — the street learns them first, so they never look suspicious for the crime of being normal.
- ✅ **It notices a stranger who keeps coming back.** Not "a stranger" — that's everyone. Someone the street doesn't know, appearing three or more times, on more than one camera, inside two weeks. That's not a passer-by. That's someone *studying* the street.
- ✅ **The camera can only ever say "look at this."** It cannot say "that's the one." It literally cannot — there is no path in the code that lets it. **Only a person can.**
- ✅ **It keeps shapes, not faces.** A face becomes a long list of numbers. You cannot turn the numbers back into a photo. There is no album of your neighbours' faces anywhere in it.
- ✅ **It hears what the camera can't see.** Rain, darkness, a mask, a wall — a camera loses. Breaking glass and gunshots have a sound that can't hide.
- ✅ **It checks itself twice.** The first listener says "that might be glass breaking." The second listener says "no — that's someone talking." **The system argues with itself and the doubter wins.**
- ✅ **When you're away and something moves in your yard, it makes noise on purpose.** Not to catch anyone — to make them leave. Nobody should be in your yard while you're gone.
- ✅ **It knows where trouble happens, and when.** 15,712 real incidents plus official police numbers for 678 police stations across all nine provinces. Which suburbs, which crimes, which direction they're heading.
- ✅ **It sends the patrol car where it will do the most good.** Instead of driving around hoping, it works out the best route, and to park for about twelve minutes at each spot — because that's the length of time that actually makes crime drop, not five minutes and not an hour.
- ✅ **It walks you home the safer way.** Not the fastest route — the one through fewer bad places at this hour.
- ✅ **It never goes blank when something breaks.** Camera loses the server? It saves up and sends later. Data is old? It *says* it's old. **It would rather tell you it doesn't know than make something up.**
- 📋 **One day, boxes on poles that talk over long distances.** Solar-powered listening posts, no cell towers needed, working out where a sound came from by which box heard it first.

## PART 3 · The blockchain — the notebook nobody can secretly rewrite

- 🔨 **Every important decision goes in a chain, where each page is glued to the one before it.** Change page 4 and pages 5, 6, 7 all break. You can see the tear.
- 🔨 **But we could still rewrite the whole book, and that's the problem.** If we own the notebook, we could redo every page and re-glue them all. You'd never know. So —
- 🔨 **Once an hour we publish a tiny fingerprint of the whole notebook where the whole world can see it.** Thirty-two characters. That's all. Now to change page 4 we'd have to change the world's copy too, and we can't.
- 🔨 **What this actually buys: proof that we wrote it down *before* we had a reason to lie.** That's the real magic. Not "unchangeable." **Provably early.**
- 🔨 **Nothing about you goes on it.** No faces, no voices, no video, no addresses. A fingerprint of a fingerprint.
- 🔨 **The most dangerous button needs two people.** Adding someone to the "belongs here" list makes them invisible to the system forever. So it takes two signatures — and it's published. **A guard who has been paid to un-protect a street can't do it alone, and can't do it quietly.** Same for switching off a camera or changing the settings.
- 🔨 **If we nearly accused you and then didn't, you can prove it.** Ask for your file. You'll see: we looked at you at 16:04, a person cleared you at 16:22, here's the proof it happened, and here's proof we haven't touched it since.
- 🔨 **You can still delete your data.** Delete the information, keep the fingerprint. *The record that a decision happened stays forever. The information about you doesn't.* The notebook never held it, so deleting yours can't break it.

## PART 4 · The whole flow, as one story

**Nomsa drives home. 19:41, N1.**

1. ✅ Her phone loses its Bluetooth link to the car. *She hasn't touched anything.*
2. ✅ Three separate senses look at it — ears, motion, car link. They agree.
3. ✅ The situation is added in: dark, load-shedding, a road where things happen.
4. ✅ The score crosses the line. Her senses aren't fighting each other, so it isn't suppressed.
5. ✅ **19:42** — her Guardian's phone lights up. A countdown starts on her screen. *She can still stop this.*
6. ✅ **19:43** — an operator sees it. The system has marked it **"worth a look"** — and **cannot go further.** A person has to decide.
7. ✅ **19:44** — the operator dismisses it. Nomsa drove into a parking garage; the link dropped. **Nothing was sent to the police.**
8. 🔨 **20:00** — that dismissal is published to the world's notebook.
9. 🔨 **Forever after** — Nomsa can prove the system considered her, a human cleared her, and nobody has edited that since.

**The most impressive thing in that story is step 7. The system decided not to accuse someone — and step 8 makes that decision permanent.**

## PART 6 · The technical depth — why this isn't a weekend project

**Scale**
- ✅ ~33,600 lines of application code across two repos
- ✅ 440 test cases · 27 accepted architecture decision records · ~6,800 lines of architecture docs
- ✅ 4 contributors, 5 documented roles
- ✅ **Measured** end-to-end latency: p50 273 ms, p95 318 ms against a 2,000 ms budget. Measured with a harness that matches by ID so a concurrent probe can't corrupt the result

**Machine learning & data science**
- ✅ On-device YAMNet TFLite acoustic classifier, sha256-registered in a model registry — and a registry bug caught by loading the real interpreter rather than trusting the recorded value
- ✅ Two-stage acoustic cascade: cheap always-on gate, expensive classifier only on trigger
- ✅ **Calibrated log-odds fusion** across physically independent channels, with a **conflict coefficient K** that suppresses escalation when sources disagree
- ✅ **Dempster-Shafer deliberately rejected** — it misbehaves under high conflict (Zadeh's paradox) and the worst cases *are* high-conflict. ADR-0005
- ✅ Hysteresis on every threshold (separate up/down) so the state can't chatter at the boundary
- ✅ **Calibration harness** — reliability diagrams, expected calibration error
- ✅ **False-alarm budget as code** — turns a decision log into false-escalations-per-user-week, checkable in CI instead of asserted by feel
- ✅ **Lazy suspicion decay** computed at read time — `0.5^(days/7)` — which solves "no scheduler in a single-process system" without changing the half-life behaviour
- ✅ 15,712 incidents cleaned with three audit flags → 764 hotspot suburbs → 709 geocoded via cached Nominatim
- ✅ Official police statistics joined at station level: 678 precincts, all nine provinces, with trend direction per crime type
- ✅ **A published held-out backtest** — and we report that the model currently loses to a constant baseline (MAE 0.484 vs 0.246). *Publishing that is the point*
- ✅ OR-Tools team-orienteering patrol routing, 12-minute Koper dwell grounded in the deterrence literature
- 🔨 Fit the fusion weights on real labelled data and publish the reliability diagram
- 🔨 Small local vision-language model for *behaviour* (climbing, loitering) — entering fusion as a **labelled-uncalibrated factor**, never a verdict

**Engineering**
- ✅ Kotlin native Android: foreground service, PARTIAL wakelock, boot receiver, sensor modules — survives screen-off, doze, and load-shedding
- ✅ FastAPI + WebSockets with room-based routing; Alembic migrations from day zero; rate-limit middleware; operator auth
- ✅ Frozen API contract requiring both builders' sign-off to change
- ✅ Contract tests that assert exact request/response **shapes**, not just status codes
- ✅ SHA-256 prev-hash evidence chain with an integrity verifier exposed as an endpoint
- ✅ Graceful degradation as architecture — every layer defines its behaviour when the layer above disappears
- 🔨 Ed25519 signing per party, Merkle batching, public anchor, subject-access endpoint

## PART 7 · The creative approach — why nobody else builds this

**Every other team will build a system that tries to be right. This one is built to be *checkable when it's wrong*.**

Five things that are genuinely unusual, and all five are already in the repo:

- **The machine has a ceiling, in code.** Not policy, not a setting — there is no path in `brain/fusion.py` that can mark a person as flagged. Only `human_verify()` can, and it requires an operator ID. **A biometric match is a lead for a human, never a verdict** — because demographic false-positive rates differ by up to a hundredfold, and in South Africa that isn't academic.

- **Whitelist before watchlist.** Learn who *belongs* first. Recurrence only counts as suspicion when the person is unknown to the street. That single inversion is what stops a domestic worker being flagged for going to work.

- **The system argues with itself and the doubter wins.** The conflict coefficient means internal disagreement *suppresses* action instead of being averaged away. Almost every other system treats disagreement as noise to smooth over. This one treats it as information.

- **An honesty ledger — a written list of claims we refuse to make.** Never "identifies criminals" (it produces leads). Never "court-admissible" (it's *structured to support a case*). Never "unbiased AI" (it's bias-*aware*, and the answer is the human gate). Never an uncalibrated number. Every simulated component carries a `sim_` prefix in the code *and* is named as simulated out loud.

- **We publish the numbers that embarrass us.** The forecast loses to a constant. The fusion weights aren't fitted yet, and the parameter file says so in machine-readable text: *"PROVISIONAL — NOT fit on real data. Do not present as tuned/validated."* One script opens by attacking its own project — *"that is descriptive analytics, not prediction, no matter how it's presented"* — and then tells the reader three ways to catch it lying.

### The standout, in one line

> **Everyone worries about who a system puts on a watchlist. The more dangerous power is who it quietly takes off.**
>
> Adding a vehicle to our "belongs here" list makes it invisible to our own suspicion engine — permanently, silently. So that action needs two signatures and it's published to a public chain. A guard who has been paid to un-protect a street can't do it alone, and can't do it without leaving a mark.
>
> No commercial camera network in the world defends against its own operators. That's the gap.

### And the closing

> Every safety network in South Africa asks you to trust it. Ours can be checked.
>
> We refused four blockchain features we designed — automated gate unlocking, first-responder bounties, reward tokens, and putting alerts on-chain — because our own architecture decisions from July forbid them. The ADR numbers are in the repo.
>
> The most autonomous thing this system does is **forget you.** Seven-day half-life, no human needed. Most surveillance systems require someone to decide to clear you. **Ours decides on its own to stop watching.**
