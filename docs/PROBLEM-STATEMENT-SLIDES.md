# VUKA — Verifiable Proof of Duress

Team SONAR · Geekulcha Annual Hackathon 2026 · Blockchain for Impact Use

---

# Slide 1 — The Problem

- A person is forced to authorise a bank transfer at gunpoint.
- The attacker takes their thumb, unlocks the banking app, transfers the money out.
- The victim performs the transaction. Every record says they authorised it — their face, their finger, their phone.
- There is no record of the coercion. Only the transaction.
- The victim has to prove they didn't do it. They can't.

---

# Slide 2 — Everyone Loses

| Who | How they lose |
|---|---|
| The victim | Loses the money. Then carries the debt and the burden of proof. |
| The insurer | Cannot tell a real claim from a fake one. Pays fraudsters or fights real victims. |
| The bank | Holds the transaction. Not the duress. Cannot resolve what it cannot see. |

---

# Slide 3 — How the Insurer Pays Fraudulent Claims

- A fraudster stages a hijacking. Claims the car was taken and the account was drained.
- They present a bank transaction showing money left their account.
- The bank confirms the transfer was authorised — the phone was unlocked, the app opened, the money moved.
- The insurer has no way to disprove it. No duress record exists.
- Fraudulent and real claims look identical on paper. The insurer pays.

---

# Slide 4 — How the Bank Loses Money

- Customer says they were forced. Bank records say the transfer was authorised.
- No proof of coercion. The bank cannot refund.
- Customer escalates to the Ombud. 743 cases a month, up 25% in a year.
- Each escalation costs R4,962 and takes 61 days.
- Fraud complaints nearly doubled. Disbelieved customers leave.

<small>Sources: NFO Annual Report 2025 (banking formal cases ≈742.83/month, +25.4% y/y); OBS Annual Report 2024, 2023 data (R4,962.38 cost per formal case); NFO via The Citizen, 16 Mar 2026 (fraud complaints ≈doubled 2024→2025; ≈61 days average to finalise a fraud complaint).</small>

# Slide 5 — Who, Where, When

- Who: Anyone whose phone is their bank. On runs, commutes, in driveways.
- Where: In cars during hijackings (44% of kidnappings). In homes. At ATMs.
- When: Any time. Peaks at midnight. Three times the early-morning rate.
- How many: ~13,600 coerced transfers a year (SAPS + ISS, calculated).
- Scale: R2.4bn lost to digital fraud in 2025 across 110,074 incidents (SABRIC 2025).

<small>Sources: SAPS via ISS Africa, 3 Dec 2024 (17,061 kidnappings 2023/24; 44% during a hijacking); SABRIC 2025 (R2.4bn gross losses, calendar 2025; 110,074 incidents — all digital banking crime, not coerced transfers). ⚑ "~13,600 coerced transfers" and the "midnight / 3×" peak are not in docs/EVIDENCE.md — confirm with Khutso before presenting.</small>

# Slide 6 — The Gap

- Every safety product ever built asks you to press a button.
- Coercion removes your free hand and your privacy.
- There is no product for the person who cannot ask.
- VUKA is that product.

---

# Slide 7 — Where VUKA Comes In

The flow:

1. VIGIL detects duress on the phone. No button. No free hand needed.
2. The record is sent to three places — phone, guardians, encrypted store — before the attacker can erase it.
3. ANCHOR fingerprints the record and publishes it to a public blockchain every hour.
4. The insurer or bank runs the math, checks the fingerprint. It matches or it doesn't.
5. A human at the institution reads the verified record and decides.

---

# Slide 8 — Why Blockchain

- Without it, the insurer must trust VUKA's database. A database is a promise, not evidence.
- With it, the insurer verifies without trusting us.
- Remove the blockchain. The business model collapses.
- Cost: R569.47/month for the entire network. Fixed. Never grows.
- No tokens. No personal data on-chain.
