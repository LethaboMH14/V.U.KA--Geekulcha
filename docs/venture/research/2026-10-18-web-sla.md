### 2.3 Azure features that change the SLA

- **App Service zone redundancy** needs Premium v2, v3 or v4 (or Isolated v2 on an App Service Environment v3), **at least 2 instances**, and a region and scale unit that support zones (`REPORTED`, search summary of Microsoft Learn: https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/reliability/reliability-app-service, https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/appsonazureblog/-/4433526). The CLI example uses `--zone-redundant --number-of-workers 2 --sku P1V3` (`FACT`: `articles/app-service/configure-zone-redundancy.md`, ms.date 2026-08-24). Basic and Standard cannot reach the 99.99% App Service SLA.
- **Health check on a single instance** (VUKA's B1): "If your app has only one active instance and becomes unhealthy, the load balancer doesn't remove it… after **one hour** of continuous unhealthy pings, the instance is replaced." At most one instance is replaced per hour and three per day. `FACT`: `articles/app-service/monitor-instances-health-check.md` (ms.date 2026-08-24). A worker-heartbeat `/healthz` on B1 would therefore **detect** a stalled worker, but the platform would take up to an hour to **replace** the instance. An in-process watchdog or an auto-heal rule is still needed.
- **PostgreSQL zone-redundant HA is not supported on the Burstable tier.** It needs General Purpose or Memory Optimized. Failover is reported at **60–120 s with zero data loss** (`REPORTED`, search summary of https://docs.azure.cn/en-us/reliability/reliability-postgresql-flexible-server and https://learn.microsoft.com/vi-vn/azure/postgresql/flexible-server/concepts-high-availability).
- **South Africa North (Johannesburg) has three availability zones, generally available since October 2021** (`REPORTED`: https://news.microsoft.com/en-xm/2021/10/13/azure-availability-zones-to-enable-competitiveness-of-sa-businesses/, https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/microsoft-adds-availability-zones-to-south-africa-and-south-korea-azure-regions). Whether App Service zone redundancy and PostgreSQL ZR-HA are offered **in South Africa North specifically** was not checked (gap).

## 5. SRE practice for a safety alert pipeline

- **Definitions** (`FACT`, OpenSLO glossary, https://github.com/OpenSLO/OpenSLO/blob/main/glossary/README.md):
  - An SLI is "a carefully defined quantitative measure of some aspect of the level of service".
  - An SLO is "a target value… measured by an SLI".
  - An SLA is "an explicit or implicit contract… that includes consequences of meeting (or missing) the SLOs".
  - The error budget is how much the SLO may be violated in a period.
- **Use time-slices, not request counts, for a help-calling system.** OpenSLO's example names "a system for reporting incidents or calling for help" and says **Timeslices fits perfectly**: count good minutes over total minutes, so a failure at 3 a.m. with one user counts as much as one at peak. `FACT`: https://github.com/OpenSLO/OpenSLO/blob/main/examples/treat-low-traffic-as-equally-important/README.md
- **Low traffic means synthetic traffic.** A pilot with a few real alerts a month cannot produce a meaningful p95. Run **canary alerts** from a synthetic member to a test guardian device, for example every 15 min, which gives about 2,880 samples a month. Report S2 with its n (`PROPOSED`, matching the house rule "method and n").
- **Burn-rate alerting.** Google's multi-window, multi-burn-rate pattern pages when 2% of the monthly budget burns in 1 h or 5% in 6 h, and raises a ticket at 10% in 3 days. `ASSUMPTION` (recalled): https://sre.google/workbook/alerting-on-slos/
- **SLA versus SLO.** Contract **fewer and looser** SLAs than the internal SLOs, and contract only what VUKA controls. Microsoft does the same: the ACS SLA excludes carriers, and the Notification Hubs SLA measures the send API (both `FACT`, §2.1). "Server dispatched the alert" can be an SLA. "Guardian's phone received it" should be a **reported SLO**, because FCM and the carriers are outside VUKA's control.
- **Probes.** Hourly GitHub Actions runs cannot see outages shorter than an hour, and scheduled runs can be delayed or dropped (`FACT`, §2.4). For S1, add a **1-minute external probe** of the heartbeat-backed `/healthz`, either Azure Monitor / Application Insights availability tests or an independent uptime service. Keep the GitHub probe as a second, independent vantage point (`PROPOSED`).
- **Multi-channel fallback** (`PROPOSED`):
  - All alerts: push first, then SMS if not acknowledged within 60 s, then a voice call at 120 s (scale phase).
  - Duress alerts: push and SMS in parallel at scale.
  - Never send from the member's phone (`docs/venture/04-SLA.md` §4.3, item 4).
- **Status page** (`PROPOSED`): a public page showing the last 90 days per SLI, fed by the probe. Host it somewhere other than the API it reports on.

1. Today VUKA has **no contractual SLA under it**: credits are excluded from Microsoft's SLA. It also runs on a subscription whose terms are non-commercial, which will be **disabled when the $100 credit runs out**, and which cannot be placed in South Africa.
2. The cheapest move with the largest effect is a **pay-as-you-go subscription in South Africa North** with the same small SKUs (about R470/month on a US basis), plus the worker heartbeat, a watchdog and a 1-minute probe. That supports an honest **99.5% pilot** promise.
3. **99.9% at scale** needs zone-redundant Premium App Service plus PostgreSQL ZR-HA, about R8–10k/month on a US basis, and a 24/7 rota.
4. Promise what VUKA controls (dispatch); measure and report what it doesn't (delivery), with n.
